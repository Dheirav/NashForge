NashForge · heads-up no-limit hold’em

What NashForge has measured

Three agent families — counterfactual regret minimisation, evolutionary search and PPO — compared on one instrument. All three are now measured against the same panel at the same bar. This page keeps what has been measured separate from what has only been run, because the project has twice mistaken the second for the first.

Status at 20 August 2026, evening · every figure below read from results/

The result: the abstraction crossover

+0.916 ± 0.118 = 7.8σ

Chips per hand to equity bucketing over made-hand bucketing, head to head at the 2560-second training budget. Three seeds, 20,000 hands per point.

The two abstractions describe a hand differently, and which one is better depends on how much compute it is given. Made-hand bucketing is roughly five times cheaper per iteration, so at equal wall-clock it takes far more of them — and early, that wins. Equity bucketing overtakes it only once both have had enough iterations for the finer description to pay for itself.

Head to head at four training budgets. Positive favours equity bucketing; iteration counts are the mean across three seeds.

The crossing itself sits at 640 seconds, where the two are not separated. That row is the finding as much as the last one is: it is where the answer to “which bucketing” stops being one abstraction and becomes the budget.

Why the solver can be believed

A CFR implementation that has converged to the wrong thing looks exactly like one that has converged. Three checks pin it down, and the third is reported here as a failure because that is what it was.

Kuhn

Has an analytically known game value of −1/18 to the first player. The solver reproduces it.

Leduc

Small enough to traverse exactly, so exploitability is computed rather than estimated, and is required to fall toward zero. It does.

No-limit

Cannot be traversed, so exploitability was to be bounded below by Local Best Response. After four defects fixed and three valuation models, LBR still could not beat a converged strategy. The investigation was closed with no usable bound — no-limit has no exploitability figure at all.

Evolutionary search: what it learned, and what did not transfer

The final genome against an untrained one from the same initial distribution, both against the same panel, both at 40,000 hands. The difference between the columns is the result; each column on its own is a fact about the opponent as much as about the agent.

Endpoint test, 40,000 hands per matchup, ±14 BB/100.
Opponent Untrained After 50 generations Difference
random −13.4 ± 14 +192.7 ± 15 +206.1 ± 39 improved
always-call −8.1 ± 14 +0.5 ± 4 +8.6 ± 28 no change
CFR −403.9 ± 13 −370.1 ± 14 +33.8 ± 37 no change

It punishes an opponent that folds and raises at random by 206 BB/100, gains nothing against a station that never folds, and is still beaten by the solver by 370 BB/100 after fifty generations. That is sharper than the audit’s prediction that the method would simply plateau: it did learn, and what it learned did not transfer.

superseded

An earlier version of the CFR row read +107.8 ± 39, improved. It was computed with a benchmark agent that missed 74.3% of its strategy lookups and was therefore playing close to at random under the solver’s name. The row above is the re-measurement after that was fixed, at a 0.0% miss rate. The miss-rate counter exists precisely so that failure shows up as a number.

PPO: what self-play learned, and that it transferred

Self-play with snapshot pooling ran three seeds to 8 million hands each on 17–18 August, with rungs kept at 500k, 2M and 8M so the comparison gets a budget axis rather than a single endpoint. Each rung was then played against an untrained policy from the same initialisation, both against the same panel, both at 40,000 hands.

−380.9 → −11.6

BB/100 against the CFR agent, untrained versus after 8M hands, mean of three seeds. All 27 rows — three seeds, three rungs, three opponents — improved.

Gain over the untrained policy from the same initialisation, in BB/100. Mean across three seeds; spread is the range across them.
Trained to vs random vs always-call vs CFR
500,000 +189.7 (233.0) +287.2 (391.4) +323.6 (134.2)
2,000,000 +214.9 (92.1) +294.1 (388.0) +387.6 (40.1)
8,000,000 +123.8 (140.8) +471.5 (609.1) +369.2 (62.3)

This is the contrast the project was built to draw. Given the same panel and the same bar, evolutionary search moved −403.9 → −370.1 against the solver and was scored no change. Both halves of the audit’s prediction now have numbers: the evolutionary method plateaus, and policy gradient does not.

The ladder is flat after 2M hands. +387.6 against the solver at 2M, +369.2 at 8M, on seed spreads of 40 and 62. Three quarters of each run’s wall-clock bought nothing that can be measured. That is a finding about how to spend a budget, not a disappointment.

Only the CFR column is quotable. Its seed spreads are 40–134 BB/100; the two baselines run 92–609. Against always-call at 8M the three seeds scored +435.1, +185.2 and +794.3 — the direction is certain and the magnitude is not resolved at all. The opponent from outside the lineage is the stable yardstick; the hand-written ones are not.

Validity: the CFR lookup miss rate was 0.0% across all nine matchups, and the untrained baselines agreed across seeds at −384.6, −381.7 and −376.3. The spread across seeds, not any single seed’s ±14, is the error bar this result is quoted with.

scope

Break-even here is against this CFR agent — six buckets, one raise per street. It is not a claim about strong solvers, and it is not an exploitability figure. Item 1 closed with no usable bound, so no-limit still has none.

What is withdrawn, and what is still open

withdrawn

Every result predating 12 August 2026. The old fitness function scored the wrong player, and the deck re-dealt the same two hands every hand. An untrained random network scored +451 BB/100 under it. Those reports are deleted from the tree and survive only in git history.

open

An externally produced number. Every strength figure here was computed by this project about itself. Slumbot is a fixed CFR strategy at heads-up no-limit behind a public API, reported in mbb/hand in published work. The first milestone is 10,000 hands with a confidence interval — any result — because it would be the first number here that someone else’s agent produced.
