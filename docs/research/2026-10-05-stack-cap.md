# Pricing the call we can make: `--capped-price`, and two guard and posterior fixes

Written 5 October 2026 on branch `stack-cap` (worktree `~/Code/PokerBot-sc`, cut from main at fbeff2c).
Three bugs from the 5 October reviews, each pinned by a strict-xfail test, are fixed here. Only the first changes
default play, so it sits behind a new flag that is off by default. Nothing was committed, no duel or burst was run,
and the full suite was not run. Later the same day the flag was extended to the short-stack table and to the reads
that weigh a price (section 1b), still off by default.

## 1. The default rule priced the arena's full bet

The arena's `to_call` is the bettor's whole raise, even when it is more than we have. We can only put in our
stack, and whatever they bet beyond it comes back to them uncalled. Two pieces of default play priced the full
number anyway:

- `fallback_choice`, the rule that answers a node no strategy stored, folds anything below the top class to a bet
  of the pot or more (`to_call >= pot - to_call`). At 8bb with 1,000 behind, facing a raise to 16,500 into a
  21,000 pot, that test sees 15,000 against 6,000 and folds the second-best class. The real call is 1,000 into
  8,000, an eighth, which the same rule calls with that class.
- "called for pot odds" calls any two cards at 10 to 1, but tested `to_call * 10 <= pot - to_call` on the full bet
  and only when they were all in. With 50 chips behind facing a shove into 35,450, the real call is 50 into 10,950
  (half a percent), and the rule folded.

The fix is `capped_call(pot, to_call, stack)` in `chipzen/player.py`: the call is capped at our stack and their
excess is taken out of the pot before anything is priced. `fallback_choice` takes an optional `stack` and prices on
the capped numbers when it is given; the Slumbot player never passes it, so it is unchanged. The pot-odds rule uses
the capped numbers too, and it also fires when our call puts us all in while they keep chips, because that call
ends the betting exactly as their all-in does, so the same odds argument holds.

Behind `--capped-price` (`ArenaPlayer.capped_price`; `scripts/chipzen_run.py`, recorded in the version frame as
`capped_price`; `scripts/chipzen_duel.py`'s flag string). It stays off because it changes what the default bot
does, and nothing goes live on an audit alone. `tests/test_slumbot.py` and `tests/test_slumbot_bridge.py`
(53 passed) confirm the shared `fallback_choice` is unchanged for the Slumbot player.

### What it would have changed: `scripts/audit_capped_price.py`

Read-only over the 576 match files in `~/Code/PokerBot/results/chipzen/matches`. The flag only matters where the
bet covers our stack, since everywhere else the capped call and pot are the arena's own. The capped price only
ever lowers the price, and both rules are monotone in it, so the flag can turn a fold into a call and never the
other way. The rule is re-run on the hand's strength class, read from the abstraction of the rung that answered
(the flat pair's side file, so no strategy is loaded), and every one of the 45 rule folds with a known rung came

### Left alone at first, and since extended

The short-stack table and the reads that compare `to_call` with the pot also priced the full bet. They were left
out of the first change and are now under the same flag; section 1b has what changed and what did not.

The price guard's write-up (`2026-10-05-price-guard.md`, section 2) says the rule fallback "prices the arena's own
pot". It does, but on the full bet, which is this bug, so that sentence is only true with `--capped-price` on.

## 1b. The second half: the short-stack table and the reads that weigh a price

Same flag, still off by default. The line drawn for each place is whether it compares a **price** with a threshold
or reads their **bet size** as a tell about their range. A price is what the call costs against what it plays for,
and that is the capped call against the pot without their uncalled excess. A size tell is what the profile counted
when it decided their big bets are value or their small ones air, and what they bet says what they hold whether or
not we can match all of it, so those reads keep the full bet. `decide()` now computes `price_pot, price_call` once
(capped under the flag, the arena's numbers off) and the price reads use them.

| place | compares | under `--capped-price` |
|---|---|---|
| short-stack table (`_short_stack_answer` into `ShortStackRanges.calls`) | equity against the shoving range with `to_call / (pot + to_call)` | capped. The table was solved as effective-stack all-ins, so the capped price is the one it was solved at |
| "their re-raise is value" | `to_call / (pot + to_call)` with 0.30 | capped. It fires only when the strategy picked a raise, and the arena offers no raise once the bet covers our stack, so in play this cannot change anything; it is capped so the read means one thing |
| "re-raise defended" (`--reraise-defence`, duel only) | equity against their re-raising range with the price plus 0.03 | the range is a tell and stays; the price is capped |
| "river bluff caught" (`--aggro-reads`, duel only) | `floor * pot` with `(1 - floor) * to_call` | capped on both sides |
| "big bet believed", "small bet called" | `to_call / (pot - to_call)` with 0.7 and 0.6 | full bet. Capping would turn a shove that covers us into a small bet and call a middling hand because their small bets are air, which is a read on a different bet |
| "shove call declined" | `to_call >= pot - to_call` | full bet: a fold-or-raise bot's pot-sized bet is a made hand however much of it we can match |
| "river bet believed", "opened into a folding blind", "three-bet into a folder", "bet into an over-folder" | no price | unchanged |
| "bluff withheld" (`--size-aware-bluffs`) | `bluff_break_even` of our raise | unchanged: it prices our raise, and already caps it at the chips either of us has |
| "priced a misread all-in" | the capped call | unchanged: it already priced the capped call on main |

`pushfold.py` itself is unchanged apart from its docstring. The player passes the capped numbers in, so the table
stays a pure function of a price.

## 2. The price guard (`--price-misread`)

Two fixes, no new flag, because the guard is already off by default.

**A bet that covers our stack is a real all-in.** `_misread` counted a history ending in all-in as misread
whenever the bettor kept chips. But the tree's all-in is the effective stack, so when their bet covers our whole
stack, the tree priced exactly the call we can make, and the guard was second-guessing a correct fold. Now a pseudo
all-in is a misread only while the bettor kept chips beyond what we can call: `opponent_stack > 0` and `to_call`
under our stack. `_misread` takes `to_call` and our stack as optional arguments; without our stack it behaves as
before. A `collapsed:` re-read is still always a misread, because the collapse changed the pot the tree sees.

**A river-solve fold is left alone.** `decide_river` builds its tree from the arena's own pot and stacks, so its
fold priced the real bet, but the guard saw `answered_history` ending in a pseudo all-in and called. The guard now
skips any decision the river solve answered. That only matters with `--river-solve`, which is off in production.

`scripts/audit_price_guard.py` now applies both conditions. Re-run on the same 576 files:

```
firings: 8, with their cards shown: 8, estimated chips from calling instead: +23,404 (+2,926 a firing)
all-in read that covers our stack (a real all-in): 0
```

**The eight firings do not change.** None of them faced a bet covering our stack, and none of the folds it reads was the river
solve's, so the fixes close two holes the logs never reached.

## 3. Posterior reads (`--posterior-reads`)

The player asks `river_never_bluffs` only after `never_bluffs` has fired. Under posteriors `never_bluffs` holds
the bluff-rate bound under 0.10, and a strong population prior lifts a zero's bound. Thirty alike bots bluffing
40 percent put 0 of 150 above the line. So an exact zero over many river bets fired neither read, where main fires
both, and the pair was stricter than main, which the posterior write-up says it never is. Now under posteriors an
exact zero over at least `HONEST_RIVER_MIN` (100) river bets satisfies `never_bluffs` too. With posteriors off
nothing changes.

## Flag-off equivalence against main

Main's live `chipzen/` (clean at fbeff2c) was copied into a scratch directory and played against this tree's, each
in its own process with the same seed, on 6,684 states rebuilt from 76 logged match files (every eighth file plus
the guard's four mr_hide and Blueprint matches). 207 of the states face a bet larger than our stack. With all flags
off on the default ladder: **0 of 6,684 decisions differ**. With the burst flags (v5xRR3 deep, `--stack-cap
--purify all --sequential-triggers --scout-reads`): **0 of 6,684 differ**.

After the second half the check was re-run the same way (the same 6,684 states and main's copy, which still
matches main's `chipzen/` file for file), in three configurations, each tree in its own process:

```
all flags off, default ladder:              flag off vs main 0 differ; flag on vs off 3 differ
burst flags (v5xRR3 deep, as above):        flag off vs main 0 differ; flag on vs off 8 differ
burst flags plus --aggro-reads and
  --reraise-defence (the duel-only reads):  flag off vs main 0 differ; flag on vs off 8 differ
```

**With the flag off nothing differs from main in any of the three.** With it on, every difference faces a bet that
covers our stack and is a fold becoming a call, in the places the audit names: the first half's three (2h2d by
"called for pot odds", 7c5d and Qc8h by the rule) and five by the short-stack table (8s5s, 4c6c, Td6d, 4sQs, 3hTs).
The logs had those table spots as rule folds, because older ladders and versions answered them; the replay plays
them on today's code, where the table answers first. Turning on the duel-only reads adds no difference, so on these
states and today's profiles their capped price never crossed a threshold.

## What gates it

The flag goes nowhere until it has a burst under its own label, and the decomposition (`chipzen_decompose.py
--label`) has been read for the rule's and the table's hands at short stacks. That is where all 24 audited spots
sit, and where a burst has the fewest hands. Main is untouched, and tomorrow's 09:00 burst plays from it.
