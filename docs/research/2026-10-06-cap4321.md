# The (4,3,2,1) tree at 70bb: what it was for, and what it buys

6 October 2026. The (4,2,1) trees the live sets play give the third raise one size, all-in, and have no fourth raise.
An opponent's sized third raise is therefore read by the bridge as all-in, and the bets after it are misread too. That
cost 29 rule hands at -1,470 in v5d's burst, and on 5 Oct it made balanced-next fold the best flush to a 9 percent
price. The (4,3,2,1) tree gives the third raise two sizes and adds a fourth raise, so the line exists. On 3 Oct a
warm (4,3,2,1) rung tied its (4,2,1) control head to head (50.5) after about 160M iterations; this note asks whether
that tie meant the tree buys nothing, or only that a head to head cannot see what it buys.

## Training past the bar

Cold, counted to 170M on the histogram 70bb recipe (18,489 s at 0.109 ms an iteration on 3 threads). The line the tree
adds, the turn after four raises, crossed the visit bar of 1,000 at about 110M (`2026-10-06-visit-bar.md`); every other
line was far above it.

## Head to head: level, as on 3 Oct

Inside v5h's ladder with only the 70bb rung swapped, against the (4,2,1) histogram rung at 60M, purified: 50.4 and
49.1 on two seeds of 5,000 matches. The cross-tree gate reads +56.0 ± 1.4 for (4,3,2,1), but that is the (4,2,1) side
missing 9.7 percent of decisions (no node for a fourth raise, answered check/call), not strength; a cross-tree gate
has to be read with its miss rate beside it. Against the 3 Oct warm (4,3,2,1) rung on the same tree, +1.7 ± 0.7.

## The hole, tested directly

**Structure, on our own logs.** The match logs hold 128 sized third raises by opponents, 23 of them in the 70bb rung's
range (59.2 to 83.7bb). (4,3,2,1) keeps all 23 as sized raises and answers them from its own tree; both (4,2,1) rungs
collapse all 23 to all-in. Its answers in that replay looked over-tight (it folded A8s, 56s, A2o and K7o to small
four-bets that a wide real range made cheap), but most of those hands reach the node with almost no own reach in the
(4,3,2,1) rung's strategy, because the live (4,2,1) rung played the earlier streets. A replay that mixes two trees
cannot judge the answer; the rung has to play the whole hand.

**Its own nodes are sane.** Facing a sized four-bet it continues with 27 to 41 percent of the hands that reach it
(big pairs, AK, AQs, AJs, KQs, 99 to 55), with 0 to 2 stronger-folds-more neighbour pairs a node.

**A scripted opponent that makes the raise.** Every archetype used to jam its preflop third raise, so no duel ever
reached the hole. The `thirdraiser` archetype (`chipzen/archetypes.py`) is the reraiser with that raise sized at half
the pot. Duplicate deals at a fixed 70bb, 20,000 deals, the same cards in every arm, no reads:

| 70bb rung | against thirdraiser | against reraiser (jams there) |
|---|---|---|
| (4,3,2,1) 170M | +86.6 ± 7.4 | +91.9 ± 7.5 |
| (4,2,1) 60M | +54.4 ± 6.7 (+56.1 with the price guard) | +55.5 ± 6.8 |
| live v5xRR3 (station exploiter) | +95.1 ± 6.9 (+95.4 with the guard) | |

So the sized raise costs the (4,2,1) rung only about 1 BB/100 against this shape (the bridge's collapse works), and
the guard adds little there. But (4,3,2,1) beats an aggressive re-raiser by 32 to 36 more than its matched (4,2,1) rung
whichever size the third raise is, which points at its richer menu at every raise level, not the third raise alone.

**Exploitability.** LBR at 70bb, 8 chunks of 8,000 hands on paired seeds, with sized raises allowed where a menu is
all-in only (`--offtree-third-raise 0.5 1.0`) and read as the bridge reads them (`--bridge-translation`):

| 70bb rung | LBR, BB/100 |
|---|---|
| (4,3,2,1) 170M | +21.6 ± 4.2 |
| (4,2,1) 60M | +51.3 ± 4.6 |
| paired difference | **-29.7 ± 8.0** |

The probes fired about 92 times a chunk on (4,2,1) and never on (4,3,2,1), so the difference is the tree's whole menu,
not the probes.

## What it means

The head to head ties because a solver opponent never steers into the lines the richer tree adds; that is what the
5 Oct action-abstraction note predicted ("expect the duel to tie; the LBR is what decides it"). Two other instruments
agree that (4,3,2,1) is much harder to exploit: less than half the LBR, and 32 to 36 BB/100 better against an
aggressive re-raiser. For a balanced set, (4,3,2,1) deep rungs would roughly halve exploitability at 70bb. For the live
exploiter set the comparison is different: its 70bb rung is a (4,2,1) station exploiter, deliberately exploitable
(LBR +97.8 on 5 Oct), and it already scores best against the re-raiser shapes (+95). The open test is a (4,3,2,1)
station exploiter, which could combine the two; a six-class one (the live recipe on the bigger tree) and a histogram
one (warm from the 170M rung) were started on the afternoon of 6 Oct, with LBR, the scripted duels, the field copies
and head to heads against the live rung to follow.

This also corrects the morning's conclusion that the deep (4,3,2,1) ladder should not be built because the head to
head tied. Whether to build it now depends on the station-exploiter result and on how many real bots re-raise like
the scripted shape; Shadow (45 percent preflop raises) and fermat53 (45 percent three-bets) this season do.
