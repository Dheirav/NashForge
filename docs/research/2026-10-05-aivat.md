# AIVAT on the arena logs (5 October 2026)

## Summary

`scripts/chipzen_decompose.py --aivat` adds a third column beside raw and all-in adjusted. It takes out the luck of
our hole cards and of every flop, turn and river card that the all-in adjustment cannot reach, against a value
function fitted on older matches and frozen before these bursts were scored. On the three bursts it removes another
9 to 14 percent of the variance on top of the all-in adjustment, so a burst's error falls by a further 5 to 7
percent:

| burst | hands | raw | all-in | AIVAT | AIVAT worth, in hands, against raw / against all-in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v5xRR3 purified (3 Oct, 07:31 to 08:16 IST) | 784 | +98 ± 55 | +70 ± 41 | +44 ± 38 | 2.07x / 1.16x |
| balanced-next (4 Oct evening, 20 matches) | 572 | -19 ± 68 | -17 ± 67 | -8 ± 64 | 1.14x / 1.10x |
| balanced-next (all 30 matches, 4 and 5 Oct) | 1,019 | +4 ± 50 | +10 ± 46 | +13 ± 43 | 1.33x / 1.15x |
| v5xRR3s10 purified (5 Oct, 11:26 to 12:32 IST) | 964 | -4 ± 46 | +9 ± 30 | +15 ± 28 | 2.70x / 1.13x |

Chips per hand ± standard error, on decision hands, value function `5e8aa4ce68af`. This is a real but small gain,
and it is the expected size. The bursts are purified, so our own decisions carry no randomness and get no term, and
what is left is the opponent's cards, which we do not see on most hands. The rest of this note says why, and what
would move it further.

The one reading that changes is the 3 October burst. Its +98 was +70 once the all-in runouts were taken out, and
it is +44 once the boards are taken out too, because it also ran well on the river (the river terms average +19 ±
9 chips a hand). So it is about 1.2 standard errors above zero, not 1.7.

## Method

AIVAT (Burch, Schmid, Moravčík and Bowling, AAAI 2018) replaces a hand's result with

    result - sum over control nodes of ( v(what happened) - E[ v over what could have happened ] )

where v is any value function fixed in advance. Each subtracted term has mean zero given everything before it, so
the estimate stays unbiased whatever v is, while a v that tracks the eventual result cancels the luck at that node.
The full method also averages over every private holding our strategy could have played the same way ("imaginary
observations"), and it puts a term on the deal of the opponent's cards. Both need things these logs do not have,
so this is the practical subset:

- **The deal of our hole cards.** E over all 1,326 holdings. Exact, because nothing else is known at that point.
- **Each flop, turn and river.** E over the cards we have not seen. The opponent's two cards are among those, so
  this is not quite the true distribution, which would need the opponent's range. That card-removal error makes the
  estimate read slightly high, by roughly 0.4 to 1 chip per turn or river term; it is measured below.
- **Our own decisions, only where the distribution we sampled from is known exactly.** A decision row qualifies if
  it carries that distribution (`probs`); the simulator writes it, the live client does not yet. The three bursts
  are purified (`purify: all`), so every action they took was certain, the term is identically zero, and none is
  computed. A distribution recovered approximately (from a rung that was not the one that decided, or before a read
  adjusted it) is never used, because a wrong distribution is exactly what gives the term a nonzero mean, while a
  zero term is unbiased by construction.
- **No term for the opponent's decisions or their cards.** AIVAT allows this for a player whose strategy is
  unknown. Their cards are known only at showdown and on our folds, and a term that exists only when the cards are
  shown would be selected on what happened after it.
- **A called all-in before the river** keeps the all-in adjustment's exact expectation over the runouts. That is
  AIVAT with a perfect value function on those chance nodes, so the heuristic terms stop at the all-in street.

Only the "all", "deep" and "short" rows are unbiased under AIVAT, because a hand's stack depth is fixed before any
card is dealt. The other rows (we jammed preflop, called a river shove, a read fired, and so on) are chosen by what
we or the opponent did after seeing cards, and conditioning on that makes the chance terms' mean nonzero inside the
row. The tool marks them with †; those cells say how the row did relative to its cards, not what the row won.

The code is `evaluation/aivat.py` (value function, hand trace, terms), called from `aivat_hands` in
`scripts/chipzen_decompose.py`. Without `--aivat` the script's output is byte-identical to the main tree's, checked
with and without `--allin-adjust` on labels v5x and v5iT2.

## The value function

    v = g_street(pot, effective stack) * (equity against a random hand - 1/2)     where contributions are equal
    v = - our contribution                                                         after a fold
    g_street = a * pot + b * effective stack

Equity is exact after the flop: every opposing holding from the unseen cards and every runout, through
`native.allin_edge` (0.08 s on the flop, 4 ms on the turn). Preflop it is a frozen table of the 169 classes, 4
million samples each, which averages to 0.5000 over all 1,326 deals. The useful property is that equity against a
random hand from the unseen cards is a martingale over the next card. So E over the next card of v is just v at the
previous street's equity, nothing has to be enumerated, and a chance term is `g * (equity now - equity before)`. A
test checks the identity exactly, by enumerating every turn and river card.

With a = 1 and b = 0, v is the hand checked down from here against a random hand (pot times equity, less our half).
That is the unfitted fallback (`--aivat-value checkdown`). The frozen default fits a and b per street, because a
control variate's best coefficient is the regression of the result on it, and the result of a strong hand is more
than its check-down value since pots grow. To keep Kim and Sandholm's rule (the value function is fixed before the
data it scores is seen, or the variance can be tuned away), `scripts/aivat_fit.py` fits only on matches that
started before 3 October 00:00 IST and excludes the three burst labels: 400 matches, 16,803 decision hands. The
target is the all-in adjusted net, with no intercept, because every feature has mean zero.

| street | a (pot) | b (effective stack) |
|---|---|---|
| deal | 3.13 ± 0.40 | 0.047 ± 0.017 |
| flop | 1.46 ± 0.11 | 0.070 ± 0.013 |
| turn | 2.39 ± 0.13 | 0.054 ± 0.021 |
| river | 1.94 ± 0.08 | 0.026 ± 0.018 |

The file is `evaluation/aivat_value.json`, digest `5e8aa4ce68af`, which the tool prints on every run and a ledger
entry should quote. In sample it takes the per-hand spread from 1,223 to 1,147 chips, while the check-down version
takes it to 1,172. I chose the fitted file as the primary before scoring any burst, and on the bursts it does a
little better than check-down (AIVAT over all-in 1.13x to 1.16x, against 1.09x to 1.10x):

| burst | check-down AIVAT | fitted AIVAT |
|---|---|---|
| v5xRR3 purified | +58 ± 39 | +44 ± 38 |
| balanced-next, 30 matches | +14 ± 44 | +13 ± 43 |
| v5xRR3s10 purified | +12 ± 29 | +15 ± 28 |

One caveat on that ordering. The file's `fitted` stamp is 5 October 17:09 IST, which is after all three bursts were
played (the last ended at 12:32 the same day). The data rule is met, because the fit read only matches that started
before 3 October and none of the burst labels, which the file's provenance records and a test checks. The claim that
the fitted file was picked as the primary before any burst was scored is a statement of intent, though, and nothing
in the repository can verify it.

## Validation

**Unit tests** (`tests/test_aivat.py`, 9 tests; run with the 5 in `tests/test_allin_adjust.py`, 14 passed in 7.6 s.
The preflop-table test skips if the frozen file is absent):

- board equity equals a brute force through the Python evaluator, which is independent of the native one;
- equity is a martingale over the next card, exactly, for a turn spot and a flop spot;
- the frozen preflop table averages 0.5 over all deals, to 5e-4;
- the hand trace reads pots, effective stacks and our contributions from the arena's history as the server writes
  it, and each action's value is priced at the chips that slot would have sent;
- a decision term's expectation under the logged distribution is zero for every possible choice;
- a purified row gets no term, and with zero coefficients AIVAT is exactly the all-in adjustment;
- an all-in hand keeps its exact runout and gets heuristic terms only up to the all-in street;
- simulated matches parse, and every decision row gets its term.

`tests/test_review_aivat.py` (20 tests, from an independent review) checks the estimator as a whole: a toy game
whose true value is an exact sum, every turn and river through one line, the removal formula against an enumerated
river, which streets get terms, the fit's exclusions, the frozen digest and the `--between` window. It found three
latent bugs, fixed on this branch before any of them touched a number: our decision rows were paired with our
actions through one queue for the hand, so a single missing row lost every later term uncounted (they are now
paired street by street, and an action with no row is counted as "our actions with no logged row"); `--between`
overwrote an explicit offset with IST; and a hand logged without our hole cards crashed `--aivat` instead of being
left out of all three columns and counted. Old and new pairing agree on all 29,399 logged hands except one, in an
unlabelled 13 September match with a missing preflop row that no label, burst or fit reads.

**Simulation where both strategies are known** (`scripts/aivat_sim.py`). A dealer with the arena's rules
(`chipzen_duel.Dealer`, the arena's blind schedule, matches played to a bust) writes logs in the client's format.
Our seat plays a mixed strategy, a softmax over the six abstract actions on a seeded equity estimate, and writes
the distribution into each row, so the decision terms are exercised. The opponent is a scripted archetype. 600
matches, seed 20261005, 22,692 decision hands. The check is that AIVAT minus all-in, which is minus the sum of the
terms, averages zero:

| opponent | hands | raw | all-in | AIVAT | AIVAT minus all-in | decision terms | AIVAT over all-in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| foldraise | 5,108 | -283 ± 26 | -261 ± 22 | -239 ± 21 | +21.5 ± 13.5 | -6.7 ± 9.6 | 1.05x |
| maniac | 5,586 | -165 ± 24 | -163 ± 21 | -156 ± 20 | +7.8 ± 12.3 | -8.0 ± 8.1 | 1.10x |
| station | 11,998 | -147 ± 10 | -141 ± 8 | -138 ± 9 | +2.9 ± 6.5 | -2.6 ± 3.5 | 0.89x |

Pooled by inverse variance, AIVAT minus all-in is +6.7 ± 5.3 chips a hand (z = 1.3) and the decision terms are
-3.8 ± 3.1 (z = -1.2), so there is no sign of bias at this resolution.

The card-removal approximation was then measured directly, since the simulator also records the opponent's cards.
The true expectation over the next card differs from ours by `g * (2 * equity now - eq(with o1) - eq(with o2)) / (n
- 2)` on each hand, and the mean of that is the term's bias. On 6,000 simulated hands it is -0.44 ± 0.17 chips per
turn term and -0.28 ± 0.28 per river term. An independent review measured E[term] under the true distribution at
-0.42 ± 0.30 per turn term and -1.03 ± 0.45 per river term.

The sign is what matters. The opponent's two cards can never be dealt to the board, while our average over the
unseen cards still counts them, and the negative mean says the cards that can actually come are slightly worse for
us than that average. AIVAT subtracts the term, so a term whose true mean is negative adds that amount back, and the
estimator reads high: by roughly 0.4 to 1 chip per turn or river term, so at most about 1.5 chips on a hand that
reaches the river and less over a burst, since many hands end earlier. That is negligible next to burst standard
errors of 28 to 64, but it is a bias in our favour and not noise, so it should not be quoted as zero. The flop's
version needs the equity of every flop through each opposing card and was not measured; there is no reason to
expect it to be a different order.

The station row is the honest warning. There AIVAT has more spread than the all-in adjustment (985 against 928 per
hand), because the value function is a poor match for that game: on 4,025 of those hands the chance terms alone
take the spread from 901 to 937, and the decision terms take it to 960 on their own. The estimate is still
unbiased, but a value function that does not track the result adds noise rather than removing it. On the real
bursts the chance terms reduced the spread in all three, which is what the fit on our own older matches was for.

## Why the gain is small, and what would move it

On these bursts the spread per hand is about 1,440 to 1,630 chips raw, 935 to 1,600 after the all-in adjustment and
880 to 1,530 after AIVAT. Our hole cards and the boards account for little of what is left. The terms' own spreads
per hand are about 120 for the deal, 170 to 200 for the flop, 180 to 250 for the turn and 190 to 390 for the river,
while the big losses are river stack-offs where the opponent held the better hand. That is the opponent's card luck,
and AIVAT can only reach it two ways, neither available yet:

1. **Imaginary observations over our own holdings**, which in the paper is most of the reduction. On a showdown,
   average the result over every hand we would have played the same way against the cards the opponent showed. That
   needs our strategy for about 1,000 holdings at every decision on the path: one rung loaded (about 500 MB) and a
   bucket lookup per holding. It also needs the reach weights to be exactly right, while the live bot's
   companions, the rule, reads and the pseudo-harmonic translation all change what was actually played. A wrong
   weight biases the base value, so this is the expensive and risky step.
2. **The opponent's cards on every hand.** The logs carry a `deck_commitment` on every hand and a `deck_reveal`
   that is null on all 576 match files. If the platform ever fills it, the deal of the opponent's cards becomes a
   chance node we can correct, and the full AIVAT partition (betting, board and the opponent's cards) becomes
   available.

One cheap change would make the decision terms usable without loading anything: have the client log the
distribution it sampled from at each decision as `probs`. This only pays on a set that is not purified, because a
purified set has no decision randomness to remove.

## Reproducing

    M=~/Code/PokerBot/results/chipzen/matches
    venv/bin/python scripts/chipzen_decompose.py --aivat --label "v5xRR3 purified" \
        --between 2026-10-03T07:00 2026-10-03T09:00 --matches-dir $M --progress /tmp/aivat.progress
    venv/bin/python scripts/chipzen_decompose.py --aivat --label balanced-next \
        --between 2026-10-04T12:00 2026-10-05T00:00 --matches-dir $M       # the 4 Oct evening slice, 20 matches
    venv/bin/python scripts/chipzen_decompose.py --aivat --label balanced-next --matches-dir $M
    venv/bin/python scripts/chipzen_decompose.py --aivat --label "v5xRR3s10 purified" \
        --between 2026-10-05T11:00 2026-10-05T13:00 --matches-dir $M

`--between` reads a time without an offset as IST and honours an explicit one, so `2026-10-03T07:00+04:00` is
05:30 IST.

    venv/bin/python scripts/aivat_sim.py --matches 600 --out /tmp/aivat_sim
    venv/bin/python scripts/chipzen_decompose.py --aivat --label sim --opponent station --matches-dir /tmp/aivat_sim
    venv/bin/python scripts/aivat_sim.py --out /tmp/aivat_sim --removal-check 6000

    venv/bin/python scripts/aivat_fit.py --matches-dir $M --before 2026-10-03T00:00   # refreezes the value function

AIVAT costs about 0.1 s a hand, mostly flop equities, so a 20-match burst takes 1 to 2 minutes and the 600-match
simulation about 15 minutes per opponent; `--progress FILE` keeps a done, elapsed and ETA line. The fit takes 13
minutes plus 85 s for the preflop table (`--table` reuses an existing one). Refitting changes the digest, and a
refit is only honest on matches older than everything it will then score.
